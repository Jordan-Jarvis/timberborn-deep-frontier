# Reinforced dams and hydroelectric turbines

User direction, 2026-10-20: concrete-reinforced turbines installed at the bottom of dams, substantial hp output, player-controlled discharge in cubic meters per second, concrete levees and concrete/metal dam blocks, and concrete construction requirements for advanced buildings.

This records implementation and verification separately. The native APIs below were inspected in installed 1.1.2.8. The original turbine model and control component are installed through the official builder. Native high-head main 100 and fresh-process reload 101 passed the two-lane single-unit flow, controls, shaft, animation, traversal and persistence checks. Main 100 reached 9,600 native hp at 3 m³/s; the final UI review showed 9,113 hp at 3.000 m³/s and 15.19 layers of head. Extended hydraulic and pump-loop acceptance remains pending.

## Player use and progression

The concrete production chain unlocks permanent water infrastructure as well as factory foundations. Solid concrete levees hold back water to a whole layer and support construction above. Concrete overflow dams retain water below a defined spill height. Reinforced concrete/metal blocks offer the same dependable water seal with the industrial appearance needed for larger dams. There is no invented stress, maintenance, heat or arbitrary stacking limit.

The turbine is a directional outlet through the lower dam wall. A player sets a maximum discharge in m³/s; the panel separately shows requested limit, actual flow, upstream/downstream water heights, available head and current hp. The requested flow is a ceiling, not a promise of water supply. Closing the outlet stops discharge and generation; reopening uses the native water simulation. Multiple units share the reservoir and cannot each claim all of its flow.

The native Pause button closes the turbine's gate and stops its rotor and generation. Resume reuses the existing manual-closure, automation and discharge settings; it cannot override a manually closed outlet or an Off signal. This uses native `PausableBuilding.PausedChanged` rather than a second pause state. Build 97 passed explicit main and fresh-process reload Pause/Resume checks, including actual native shaft output and rotor movement.

The 2026-10-20 sound/motion revision uses the native Wind Turbine's `Gristmill` mechanical loop instead of `WaterWheel`. The existing `PowerGeneratorSoundsSpec` controls playback through the native mechanical node and building audio mixer; no second audio controller is added. The catalog's required `animationSpeedMultiplier: 4` makes both the runner and coupled shaft animate four times faster at the same measured flow and simulation speed. The animation still stops at zero output. This presentation change does not alter discharge or hp. Fresh-process build 103 measured 4.00× playback and nonzero native spatial audio output at 0.75 and 3 m³/s, producing 2,400 and 9,600 native hp. Building Pause and closure disabled animation and faded the source to zero volume; Resume restored operation. Evidence: `outputs/hydro-continuity103.json`, `outputs/hydro-reload-measurements103.json` and `outputs/hydro-reload103.log`. Earlier 100/101 results cover the hydraulic tests, separately from this audio/motion revision.

Use actual forward through-flow and positive upstream/downstream head for generation. Dry intake, closed outlet, blocked discharge, equal water levels and reverse flow must not produce fictional power. Water and contamination pass downstream; the turbine does not consume water as an inventory ingredient, cleanse badwater or create a second fluid simulation. Flooded electrical behavior and badwater compatibility must be explicitly authored and tested.

Initial balance direction is a large renewable power source for an established industrial district, with major dam construction and watershed dependence. A good site should serve several factories; nuclear retains its compact 75,000 hp planning role and its continuous coolant/waste obligations. The user has now specified the turbine rating below. Preserve that requested tuning while testing actual native and dependency pump loops; do not silently alter pump costs or call the result balanced before measuring it. There is no fuel requirement or routine servicing.

## Native 1.1.2.8 contracts

- `ThrottlingValve` owns native discharge controls, automation, synchronization and persistence. Its `ApplyCurrentOutflowLimit` calls `IWaterService.SetInflowLimit` despite the player-facing outflow terminology. It adds a directional limiter at its registered coordinate and closes the outlet through `WaterObstacleController` at zero. Reuse this control path if the final outlet geometry fits its single-coordinate contract.
- The native Iron Teeth throttling valve has a 2 m³/s maximum and 0.01 increments. These are vanilla values, not a confirmed final turbine capacity. Its spec and UI are in the shipped `Buildings/Landscaping/ThrottlingValve` blueprint and `Timberborn.WaterBuildings`.
- `IThreadSafeWaterMap` exposes `WaterDepth`, `WaterFlowDirection`, `WaterHeightOrFloor`, `TryGetColumnFloor` and per-height columns. Read the actual intake/outlet columns, not the highest unrelated water surface. Confirm flow vector semantics at the submerged outlet before using it for hp.
- `IWaterService` supplies full, horizontal and partial obstacles plus directional/inflow limits. Native `Dam` uses a 0.65-height partial obstacle; native `Levee` uses a full one-layer obstacle. Neither is a reservoir-volume inventory.
- `MechanicalNode` and its output multiplier integrate generation into native shafts and Power Lines. The native large Iron Teeth wheel authors 540 output and zero input; its output is flow-dependent, so 540 must not be described as a fixed yield or directly compared to turbine peak hp.
- Native localization uses `m³/s` and `hp/m³/s`. Preserve those units. Inventory Water and Timber Pipes volume conversion are separate contracts.

Evidence: `outputs/civic-native-api/WaterBuildings.cs`, `outputs/navigation-api-1.1.2.8/Timberborn.WaterSystem.cs`, and shipped blueprints/localization under `vendor/timberborn-modding/Assets/Tools/ImportedAssets/Editor/Resources`.

## Installed Compact Water Turbine inspection

Workshop package `3750784853`, `Calloatti.CompactWaterTurbine` 1.7.2, is already enabled. Its Iron Teeth module is 1×3×1, costs 50 gears / 25 treated planks / 25 metal blocks, and unlocks at 4,000 science. Its blueprint authors 1,000 hp output and zero demand; the component defaults are 0.5 m³/s maximum, one-layer minimum head and five-layer full-output head. These are inspected package values, not our proposed turbine balance.

It exposes public flow/synchronization controls and saves them. It transports clean and contaminated water between explicit world coordinates through `IWaterService`; it is not the native throttling valve. Its `MoveWater` returns the amount available for transfer, but `Tick` ignores that return when calculating power and animation from the requested/ramped flow. This source finding needs runtime reproduction before labeling it a confirmed gameplay bug, but it prevents treating its reported effective flow as verified delivered water. Its custom synchronizer and panel also specifically target its component.

Do not silently copy or patch that package. Deep Frontier uses native physical water services and the native automation interface, with its own explicit aperture coordinate and persisted discharge setting. Its generator reads actual directional through-flow. The installed turbine remains a comparison and possible future collaboration target. Decompiled inspection: `outputs/compact-water-turbine-1.7.2/compactwaterturbine.decompiled.cs`; original package remains unchanged.

`HydroControlProbe.cs` is a separate command-line-gated experiment, launched by `tools/start_hydro_probe.ps1`. It builds a tall concrete test reservoir around a native valve and records requested/current limits, water surfaces, reservoir volume, flow vectors and directional outflows at 0 / 0.25 / 1 / 0 m³/s. It does not create turbine generation. Its measurements and compilation/runtime status belong in `tools/STATUS.md`; source presence alone is not acceptance evidence.

Detailed native outflow reads are singleton-Tick-only. Probe 90's UpdateSingleton read was rejected by `TickOnlyArray`; build 91 moves sampling into `ITickableSingleton`. Any gameplay use of this service must share one outflow snapshot among all turbines. A full-world copy per building is unnecessary.

Native `FlowDirectionExtensions` maps Cw0 to +Y (Top) and Cw180 to -Y (Bottom). Probe 91 accidentally faced into its reservoir and measured zero flow. Corrected native probe 92 passed: settled maximum discharge was 0 / 0.249844179 / 0.999844134 / 0 m³/s for the four requested phases. Water left the finite reservoir. Its directional outgoing flow matched the physical discharge; the flow vector did not need a divide-by-two conversion. Preserved evidence: `outputs/hydro-control-probe92.json`, `outputs/hydro-probe92.log`; native screenshot in the hydro review directory. These are valve measurements, not turbine generation.

Native `ThrottlingValve` places its limiter at the root `BlockObject.Coordinates`, with no authored aperture offset. The large conduit needs an interior aperture. `HydroTurbine` therefore registers its exact seal, horizontal floor/ceiling, directional limiter and gate through native water services. `HydroTurbineSimulation` shares one singleton-Tick outflow snapshot and uses the exact probe columns, including pressure from the native `OverflowPressureFactor`. `IAutomatableNeeder` supplies native wiring; manual closure and the discharge setting are persisted by the component. A native placement validator requires both probes inside the map.

The controlled crossing is the incoming edge from the upstream neighbor into the aperture. In native `OutflowsUpdateTask.Outflow`, the target column's floor selects its inflow limit. Run 95 sampled the aperture's outgoing edge instead and recorded a 0.3636943 m³/s pressure pulse at a 0.25 setting. Build 96 corrects the sampling coordinate; the meter still reports an actual native outflow, never the requested value substituted as a measurement. Native main 96 passed actual maxima of 0.25, 1 and 3 m³/s in the corresponding phases, finite reservoir drawdown and actual rotor/shaft generation. Manual and automated closure stopped operation; a plugged outlet settled to zero flow and power. Five actual beaver destinations also passed. Build 97 added native Pause/Resume coverage and passed both the full main sequence and fresh-process reload, including all 211 identities/positions, saved settings/wiring, actual shaft output, rotor movement and five walking destinations. Both completed native saves passed capture verification.

Balance testing must also cover the chosen transport dependencies. Inspected Timber Pipes 11.4.0 horizontal/vertical pumps cost 50 hp and supply two layers of rated head lift. Their default rate is one inventory good per tick (0.2 m³), not one m³/s. Real delivered flow depends on the network and native tick duration. Compare actual pump loops before choosing a turbine coefficient; do not silently alter dependency costs or pretend a source setting is measured throughput.

## Construction gates and visual design

Concrete is required for advanced industrial, power, research, civic and launch foundations. Keep the initial pits, crusher, kiln and mixer buildable before the colony has concrete; otherwise the chain cannot start. Permanent quarries and later machines may require it. Change costs in their canonical catalogs/generators and regenerate the workbook; do not patch generated blueprints alone.

Reuse the accepted Iron Teeth palette: cast pale concrete, heavy native metal bands, bolted turbine housings, an enclosed generator room, arched service door, accessible control deck and a visibly continuous intake-to-outlet passage. The rotor and outlet flow should follow actual operation. Deck heights and seals use whole grid layers.

The canonical catalog is `tools/hydro_content.json`: 8,000 science (lowered from 30,000 on 2026-10-21 to close the pre-reactor power gap), 160 Concrete, 100 Metal Blocks, 60 Gears and 40 Treated Planks. It has zero workers and zero power demand. The user's revised rating is **9,000 hp at 3 m³/s and 15 layers of head**, reaching **9,600 hp at 16 layers**. Each lane contributes `200 × measured forward m³/s × positive head`, with its flow capacity capped at 1.5 m³/s and generating head at 16 layers. These caps limit machinery output, not dam height or stacking. A player-set 3 m³/s means 3 total, shared equally between both native inflow limits. Each channel's actual gate crossing and reservoir head are measured independently; reported operating head is weighted by delivered flow.

Head means upstream minus downstream water level, including native pressure. The tail-pressure probes are in the last clear conduit cells, immediately before discharge; a fully obstructed external neighbor has no water column and cannot represent pressure behind it. Rising tailwater reduces generation. Native main 100 measured 3 m³/s, 9,600 native shaft hp and both wet mouth cells, then reached a stable zero-flow/zero-power interval after the blocked conduit filled and pressurized. The new coefficient exceeds the idealized Timber Pipes pump bound described below; this requested rating is implemented without silently altering dependency costs. Actual closed-loop balance is still unresolved and must be measured before release. Existing experimental one-cell turbine placements must be rebuilt in a compatible layout: no automatic resize or save repair is added.

## Required acceptance

Test native rotation, submerged placement, upstream and downstream seals, closed/open/limited flow, finite reservoir drawdown, water and contamination conservation, positive-head output, head loss, reverse flow, drought, blocked outlet, multiple neighboring turbines, native shaft/Power Lines demand, native automation, pause/demolition lifecycle and exact save/reload continuity. Measure pump/turbine round-trip energy with actual native pump inputs and output flow. Capture models and operating panels in game. Until those checks pass, report the turbine as planned or in development.

Native basin run 105 tested two turbines in one sealed upper reservoir and a sealed downstream collection basin. Independent 0.75 and 1.5 m³/s limits produced 2,400 and 4,800 actual native hp. Clean and 50%-contaminated windows passed full-column water accounting with native evaporation included: residual water error was −0.113 m³ in about 3,205 m³, while upper-reservoir drawdown versus integrated gate flow differed by 0.675 m³ within a declared 2 m³ sampling allowance. Downstream contamination remained 50.047%, with −0.016 m³ contamination-volume residual. Both native nodes produced zero power throughout the reverse-head and dry-intake observation windows. No physics or rating correction was needed.

The explicit `-deepFrontierHydroBasins` driver implements that isolated two-unit test in the marked disposable settlement. `tools/start_hydro_basins.ps1 -Build <number>` loads the exact earlier mineral-pit fixture and prepares a separate sealed test area through native terrain and building services. It records every column's water plus overflow, contamination-weighted volume, and native evaporation rates; integrates gate flow over the same observation window; then checks contaminated transfer, higher tailwater and drained intakes. The capture tool checks real native node output and archives the completed native save. Evidence: `outputs/hydro-basins-verification105.json`, `outputs/hydro-basins-measurements105.json` and `outputs/hydro-basins105.log`. All 496 created entities and both saved controls passed capture; fresh-process reload of this particular two-unit fixture has not been tested. The approved model and rating are unchanged.

Pump-loop acceptance is separate from that basin test. First use an actual Iron Teeth Deep Mechanical Pump, with its native 500 hp maximum input and 0.25 m³/s setting, to return discharged water uphill. Then test Timber Pipes pumps and their real world-water intake/output chain. Integrate native pump hp and turbine hp over the same settled interval, accounting for the change in stored reservoir water/head; an initially charged reservoir must not be mistaken for energy created by the loop. Test several lifts and lower flow settings, not only a full-capacity pump. Do not raise the generation coefficient on the strength of its idealized source bound alone.
