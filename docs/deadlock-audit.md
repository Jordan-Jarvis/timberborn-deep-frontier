# Deadlock audit

**2026-11-04 update:** D3/D4 no longer depend on slow deep-ore recovery for construction. The Deep Mine uses Metal Blocks instead of Deep Iron/Steel, and both stone quarries use only vanilla construction goods. All three outcrop types remain when empty and recover 20 units in five days. D7 now uses Scavenger Flag extraction of Raw Deep Ore, native hauling, and a drill recipe of 1 raw ore + 3 Water -> 1 Deep Ore; drill operating supplies are Metal Blocks and Treated Planks. Existing/custom maps warn when source types are absent; supported built-in new games place them. The earlier analysis below is historical. Current contracts are in `docs/source-authoring.md`, and generated graph validation passes with zero accepted deadlocks.

Status 2026-11-03. User request: "Let's find anywhere the user can deadlock themselves and change the recipes up a bit. Please look through." Static audit of the generated Iron Teeth economy (vanilla plus Deep Frontier): recipes, construction costs, workplace upkeep, goods-consuming buildings, specialist training, food services, map resources and unlock order. One deadlock is fixed (D1). The rest are verified safe or proposed for a decision. D3 and D4, first accepted by design, were closed the same day by the recharge of empty Deep Ore Sources (docs/progression.md "Deep Ore Source recharge"). The user approved the three proposals the same day, and all three are made: P1 fixes D5, P2 fixes D6, and P3 puts the Deep Mine's shaft lining on Brick. None of it has been played in game; `tools/STATUS.md` records runtime verification.

The check is `tools/validate_deadlocks.mjs` (a validate_all check, `deadlocks`). It covers what `tools/audit_progression.mjs` does not model, so it does not repeat the audit's construction-good, recipe-input and bootstrap checks.

### D1. Deep Mine drill bits (fixed)

- **Chain:** Deep Ore ← Deep Mine ← Steel drill bits (0.1 per full-crew hour; production stops without them, `WorkplaceUpkeep.cs`) ← Steel Mill ← 2 Deep Iron + 2 Coal + 1 Limestone ← Deep Ore Processor ← Deep Ore. One steel embodies about 2.33 deep ore.
- **How a player gets there:** the mine burns its steel stock (15 units at the mine) while the finite Deep Ore Sources are spent and no steel is left in storage, for example after spending it on steel structures, rockets or bot frames. The mine shows "Waiting for drill bits" and nothing can ever make steel again, so Alloy, Machine Cores, Electronics, Power Modules, fuel, rockets and the reactor all stop.
- **Specialists** (coordinator brief): the same loop ran through training. Metallurgy needs Deep Iron; Deep Iron needs the Deep Mine; the mine needed Steel; Steel needs metallurgists at the Steel Mill. If the metallurgists died (the Steel Mill is Extreme danger, 2% of injuries fatal) after the sources were spent, they could not be retrained.
- **Fix (`tools/workforce_risks.json` `workplaceUpkeep`):** the Deep Mine wears **0.3 Metal Block** per full-crew hour (0.1 Steel by raw effort: 0.1 × 11.45 / 4.2 = 0.27, rounded up), with its 0.1 Concrete shaft lining unchanged (0.1 Brick since P3, below). This is the change the Deep Drill got on 2026-10-31 for the same reason. Metal Block is renewable from the Efficient Mine (4,000) and the Smelter, and needs no deep ore. The role `SteelDrillBits` is removed; the mine uses `DrillBits` ("Waiting for drill bits: production has stopped until haulers bring metal blocks."). Description: "Uses up metal blocks as drill bits and concrete as shaft lining; stops without them." ("bricks as shaft lining" since P3) The mine's 15-unit stock now lasts 50 full-crew hours (150 with steel).
- **Steel keeps ongoing uses:** the Deep Well's drill bits, the reactor's casks (1 per 2-hour cycle), rocket airframes and propulsion, reinforced bot frames, Reactor Bot plating, Alloy, and steel structures.
- **Saves:** a saved steel part-unit at a mine is dropped with a log warning (`WorkplaceUpkeep.Load`). Steel in the mine's stock becomes a leftover that haulers carry out (`BotMaintenanceInventory` and Moddable Recipes' leftover rule). The Deep Drill change of 2026-10-31 used the same path. Not tested in game.

### D2. Technical School programs and specialist-only buildings (verified)

The coordinator's rule: no training supply may depend on a building that needs that same specialist. The beaver-specialist path must work on its own, so the 0.8.5 Reactor Bot bypass (any workplace can be set to Reactor Bots) is not counted.

| Discipline | Needed by (science) | Supplies (`workforce_risks.json` school) | Producers (crew) | Trainable from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Metallurgy | Steel Mill (10,000) | Metal Block, Deep Iron, Corn Ration | Smelter; Deep Ore Processor ← Deep Mine (open crews; Metal Block and Concrete upkeep since D1); Food Factory | 2,200 (school; Deep Iron from 1,200) |
| Nuclear | Nuclear Reactor (1,000,000) | Electronics, Yellowcake, Medicine | Precision Machine Works; Uranium Processing Plant; Public Health Lab (all open or beaver crews) | 300,000 |
| Flight | Launch Pad (200,000) | Electronics, Recovered Telemetry, Canned Fish | Precision Machine Works; **Mission Control's Guidance telemetry** (beaver crew, 75,000; the Launch Pad's own copy of the recipe needs flight engineers and is not needed); Food Packaging Works | 75,000 |

Recovered Telemetry before the first launch: Mission Control (75,000, beaver crew) makes Guidance Computers (4 Electronics, 2 Machine Cores, 2 Recovered Artifacts, 1 Grease) and Guidance telemetry (1 Guidance Computer, 2 Recovered Artifacts, 1 Metal Part). Its artifacts come from the Archive Excavation (25,000, open crew) or the Human Record Caches, so neither needs a flight engineer. The validator trains a discipline only from goods available without it, and fails if any program's goods need its own discipline (fault injection: Metallurgy needing Steel, and Mission Control without Guidance telemetry, both fail).

### D3. The Deep Mine is built from finite deep ore (resolved 2026-11-03)

- **Chain:** the Deep Mine costs 80 Deep Iron (20 of them the gate that keeps the permanent mine behind the drill phase) and 60 Steel. Both come from deep ore, which is renewable only from a running Deep Mine.
- **First mine:** built from drilled ore. The map check (`prepare_deep_frontier_map.py` `finiteDeepOreMargin`) holds the Deep Ore Sources at or above the deep ore of every building up to the mine plus 5%: 1,437 (1,509 with the margin) against 17,000-18,000 on every built-in map.
- **Stuck case:** losing the last Deep Mine (a meltdown blast within 6 tiles, or deconstruction) after the sources are spent, with no Deep Iron and Steel in storage. A second mine is always built from the first (every map has at least two veins with mine sites).
- **Resolved:** an empty Deep Ore Source recharges 20 raw deep ore every 5 days (`MineralDepositSpec`), so a lost last mine (300 deep ore) can be rebuilt from the trickle: about 38 days on a two-source map, 25 on three. Deep ore is now a slow source in `validate_deadlocks.mjs`: renewable only through the recharge. The check `slow-replacement` requires the Deep Mine to be buildable and runnable at its unlock from the recharge alone. The two accepted entries failed as stale and are gone.

### D4. Quarries and the Deep Mine's concrete lining (resolved 2026-11-03)

- **Chain:** each quarry costs 35 Deep Iron. Renewable Deep Iron comes from the Deep Mine, whose shaft lining is Concrete, and Concrete is 3 Sandstone + 2 Cement (3 Limestone + 1 Brick). With the pits spent, that stone is renewable only from the quarries.
- **Stuck case:** the last Sandstone (or Limestone) Quarry is lost after both the outcrops and the Deep Ore Sources are spent, with no Concrete and no Deep Iron in storage.
- **First build is fine:** the quarries (9,000) come before the mine (15,000) and take drilled Deep Iron.
- **Resolved:** Deep Iron is renewable without the mine, from recharged deep ore (35 Deep Iron, 70 deep ore: about 9 days of a two-source map's recharge). The accepted entries failed as stale and are gone. Proposal P3 is no longer needed for D4; it would still let the mine run while the quarries are down.

### D5. Contaminated water before any consumer (fixed, P1)

- **Chain:** the radioactive tide (optional weather; Normal: from cycle 15, 25% per cycle) irradiates water and banks, and stored Water in irradiated barrels and tanks turns into Contaminated Water (`docs/radiation.md`). That storage cannot be deleted while it holds the good (`HazardousStorageDeletionBlocker`), and carrying it irradiates beavers (0.5% per carrying hour).
- **No consumer early:** nothing takes Contaminated Water before the Radioactive Waste Export (300,000). The refinery's two recipes come at 600,000. The Radioactive Water Outfall (50,000, unlocked with the Hospital "so tide water can be cleaned up before the nuclear stage") releases only Radioactive Water goods.
- **Modelling gap:** `audit_progression.mjs` `MAP_SOURCES` dates Contaminated Water from the refinery (600,000), so the audit reports the export's recipe as a late input. In play it can exist at any science once tides are on.
- **Effect:** the tanks and barrels stay full and cannot be removed or reused for about 250,000 science. Production keeps running.
- **P1:** add a recipe to the Outfall, "Release contaminated water: 30 Contaminated Water → nothing, 1 h" (`radiation_content.mjs`). The outfall releases its fixed 0.6 radioactive volume per cycle, as for 3 Radioactive Water goods; 30 matches the refinery's decontamination, where 10 Contaminated Water hold the radiation of one Radioactive Water good. It needs no new good, building or code. **Taste:** the survival design says contaminated waste leaves the map (the export), but the outfall already pours Radioactive Water into a river. User decision.
- **Made (user approved, 2026-11-03):** the outfall runs "Release contaminated water" (`ReleaseContaminatedWater`: 30 Contaminated Water, 1 h, no products; display name "Contaminated water", like the export's recipe), beside its radioactive-water recipe. Both pour the outfall's one `RadioactiveVolumePerCycle` (0.6): `radiation_content.mjs` derives 30 = 3 × `CONTAMINATED_WATER_PER_SLUDGE` (10) and throws if an outfall recipe releases another volume; `validate_radiation_content.mjs` checks every outfall recipe against the generated refinery recipe (10 contaminated water in, 1 radioactive water out) and fails without the contaminated-water recipe (fault injection: 20 per cycle fails). Inventory: 5 cycles, 150 contaminated water; pipes as before (valve set to Fill).
- **Unlock against the tide:** the outfall unlocks at 50,000 with the Hospital and the recovery pump, the stage the 2026-11-01 science curve set ahead of radioactive tides (cycle 15 by default; the Hospital comes about three weeks of game days after the first Materials Laboratory). A tide that comes earlier still leaves its contaminated water stored until 50,000, as it leaves irradiated beavers untreated until the Hospital. From then on the tank-delete blocker is no trap: empty the storage into the outfall, then delete it. The blocker's message now reads "... move the contents to other storage, or pour them out, refine or export them." Description: "Pours radioactive water or contaminated water out onto the map. ..."
- **Check:** `hazard-sink` in `validate_deadlocks.mjs` (below). It reads the undeletable goods from `RadiationTuning.HazardousStockGoodIds` and dates contaminated water from the Hospital's unlock (the tide-ready stage), not from the refinery as the audit's `MAP_SOURCES` does. Radioactive Waste appears and is exported at 300,000; Radioactive Water appears (recovery pump) and is poured out at 50,000; Contaminated Water appears at 50,000 and is now poured out at 50,000 (before P1: 300,000, which fails).

### D6. Reactor Bots before the Reactor Charging Bay (fixed, P2)

- **Chain:** the Bot Assembler's Reactor bot recipe and the retrofit need Reactor Power Cells (Uranium Processing Plant, 300,000). Reactor Bots charge only at the Reactor Charging Bay (400,000; they never use the vanilla Charging Station). The bay's own cost (2 Reinforced Bot Frames, 2 Reactor Power Cells, Enriched Fuel as fuel) is available from 300,000.
- **Effect:** Reactor Bots made between 300,000 and 400,000 run out of charge (the critical charge state: the native out-of-energy pose and -3 wellbeing) with nowhere to charge until the bay unlocks. This recovers once the bay is unlocked, so it is not a hard deadlock.
- **P2:** move the Reactor Charging Bay from 400,000 to 300,000 science, with the plant that makes the cells. It is a declared upgrade of the Charging Station, so the menu-order rule keeps it beside the station. Its cost tier (T5) and its power (exempt, 5,000 hp) do not change. Taste: the 2026-11-01 science curve placed it at 400,000 on purpose.
- **Made (user approved, 2026-11-03):** `tools/super_bots.json` `station.science` 400,000 → 300,000, beside the Uranium Processing Plant (`frontier_progression.json` `nuclearScienceRule` text updated). Consequences checked:
  - **Cost unchanged:** still T5 (above 75,000), and its material world is the same, since no building unlocks between 300,000 and 400,000. Canonical generation changes only its `ScienceCost`, `material_cost_context.json` its science, and `lategame_costs --write` leaves the catalog byte-identical (15 Alloy, 10 Machine Cores, 5 Electronics, 5 Power Modules, 80 Concrete, 2 Reinforced Bot Frames, 2 Reactor Power Cells). Its cost goods all come from the 300,000 plant or earlier (`audit_progression` unlock-order and bootstrap checks pass).
  - **Power:** exempt, 5,000 hp, unchanged.
  - **Menu:** it stays right after the vanilla Charging Station in Science as the declared upgrade pair (`validate_tool_order.mjs` passes, order unchanged).
  - **Last unlock:** the Nuclear Reactor (1,000,000) stays the single last unlock (`audit_progression` `reactor-not-last` passes).
  - **Science to finish the nuclear stage** falls by 100,000: buying every nuclear building less the mission payouts is 2,050,000 (was 2,150,000), about 38.9 days at 52,640 a day (was 40.8).
- **Check:** `reactor-bot-charge` in `validate_deadlocks.mjs` (below): Reactor Bots from 300,000 (the assembler's recipe), charging from 300,000 (before P2: 400,000, which fails).

### D7. Deep ore items (done 2026-11-03, docs/progression.md "Deep Ore Source recharge")

1. Done: the Deep Drill's recipe is 3 Water → 1 Deep Ore, each cycle's ore bored out of a Deep Ore Source in its district. The source is a deposit, no longer a stockpile haulers empty.
2. **Deep ore spent outside the path to the Deep Mine** is not in the finite budget, which counts one of each building:
   - steel structures from 11,250 science: columns cost 20-120 Steel, the 3x3 decks 108 (about 250 deep ore each);
   - the Coal Power Plant from 10,000: 1 Coal per hour, a third of a deep ore, about 8 a day at full load;
   - Metallurgy training: 0.75 Deep Iron per hour while anyone studies, 1.5 deep ore.

   The sources hold more than 15,000 beyond the 1,437 the path needs, so only deliberate heavy spending strands a settlement before the mine. Since D1, a running mine no longer needs ore-made goods, so once it runs the remaining stock no longer matters. If the stock is spent anyway, the recharge (8 deep ore a day on two sources, 12 on three) is what is left. It still reaches the mine (300 deep ore, about 38 days on two sources) and the Steel Mill (270). But one coal plant at full load day and night burns about the whole recharge of a two-source map, a 3x3 steel deck takes a month of it, and each metallurgist trained (16 study hours at 0.75 Deep Iron) takes 24 deep ore, three days of it. Spending off the path is the colony's choice; the finite budget stays one of each building up to the mine and ignores the recharge.
3. D3: resolved by the recharge (above).
4. The quarries' 35 Deep Iron (9,000) come before the mine. A settlement that spends all deep ore before 9,000 and then its pit stone has no quarries, so no concrete, so no Deep Mine (725 Concrete). This double exhaustion is covered by the budget, and the recharge resolves it: 70 deep ore, about 9 days on two sources.
5. Decided: `validate_deadlocks.mjs` classifies `DeepOre` as a slow source (`MAP_SOURCE_KIND` `slow`), renewable only through the recharge. It must agree with the data: a good is slow exactly when a map-editor deposit of it recharges. The check `slow-replacement` requires a real supply (the Deep Mine) reachable from the recharge alone. The Deep Drill's recipe counts only as that map source, never as a free recipe. The Deep Drill's own upkeep (0.1 Metal Block) is renewable, and the map tool still fails if its bits ever embody deep ore.

## Model and checks (`tools/validate_deadlocks.mjs`)

- **Operating goods:** a building runs only with its workplace upkeep (`FrontierWorkplaceUpkeepSpec`), the goods its native consumer uses (`GoodConsumingBuildingSpec`: fuel and supplies) and the Deep Well's bits (`DeepWellSpec`). A specialist-only building (`FrontierSpecialistWorkplaceSpec`) also needs its discipline to be teachable, from a school program (`FrontierSpecialistSchoolSpec`) whose supplies are available. These gate all of the building's recipes, so a good never comes first from its own building's output.
- **Map sources:** each `MAP_SOURCES` good is classified finite (ruin scrap, pit stone), slow (deep ore: renewable only through the Deep Ore Sources' recharge, checked against the deposits that recharge) or renewable (shore fishing, contaminated water as a waste). A new map source fails until it is classified. A map source building's recipe that makes its own map good (the Deep Drill's) counts only as the map source.
- **renewable-economy:** every building standing and no finite source. Every good the economy uses must still be made: recipe inputs and fuel, operating goods, construction goods, the supplies of every required discipline, and enough foods for the restaurant and grocery. Before D1, 25 goods failed, every one behind "DeepOre.Mine@DeepMine (needs HighGradeSteel to run)".
- **discipline-trainable:** every required discipline can be taught from renewable goods without itself.
- **operating-unlock:** at each Deep Frontier building's unlock, its operating goods, discipline and foods are producible (buildings unlocked by then, built from producible goods, finite sources allowed, without the building itself).
- **rebuild:** with every other building standing and no finite source, each building's construction goods are made without it (goods already failing renewable-economy are not repeated).
- **finite-replacement:** for each finite map good, a renewable producer of it can be built and run at its own unlock without that finite source (scrap: Efficient Mine 4,000; Sandstone and Limestone: quarries 9,000).
- **slow-replacement:** for each slow map good, a producer other than its map source can be built and run at its own unlock from the recharge alone, with every finite source spent (deep ore: Deep Mine 15,000).
- **hazard-sink:** storage holding a good of `RadiationTuning.HazardousStockGoodIds` cannot be deleted, so each needs a consumer from its first unit: a produced good from its earliest producer (closures by unlock, finite sources allowed), contaminated water (radiation in stored water) from the Hospital's unlock, the stage the design puts ahead of radioactive tides (D5).
- **reactor-bot-charge:** the building serving the charge need (`FrontierReactorBotSettingsSpec.ChargeNeedId`, through its `AttractionSpec`) is usable no later than the first Reactor Bot (the Bot Assembler's `FrontierReactorBotAssemblySpec` recipe; the retrofit takes the same two parts) (D6).
- **Accepted** (`ACCEPTED`, with reasons): none since 2026-11-03. The D3 and D4 entries failed as stale once the recharge made deep ore renewable. An entry fails as stale when it stops failing.
- **Fault injection** (mirror only; each fails): Deep Mine on Steel bits; Metallurgy needing Steel; Mission Control without Guidance telemetry; Deep Drill on Steel bits (operating-unlock at 1,000); Coal Power Plant at 1,000 science; Deep Ore Processor costing Deep Iron; a grocery list with only 2 foods available at its unlock; the outfall without its contaminated-water recipe (hazard-sink, on HEAD 70081b3's data); the bay at 400,000 (reactor-bot-charge, the same data). Unchanged data passes.
- `node tools/validate_deadlocks.mjs [dataRoot] [--print]`; `--print` lists each discipline's training route, each finite good's renewable producers, each hazardous good's first unit and first sink, and when Reactor Bots and their charging begin.
- **Out of scope:** rates, workforce, power supply, water and radiation at runtime, map placement.
